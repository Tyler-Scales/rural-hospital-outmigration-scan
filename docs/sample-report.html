Keeping care local

Where AI patient engagement and a digital front door can keep patients from leaving the county, and what each move is worth under critical access hospital payment rules.

Public-data scan, 2026-11-02. Built from CMS and Census files and the hospital's public website and cost reports. No internal hospital data was used. Figures are estimates. The hospital and nearby hospitals are de-identified.

The short answer

Most county residents who need a hospital bed go to Referral Hospital B, 38 miles away. In 2025 it took 266 of the 388 Medicare stays we can count from the county's ZIP codes (69%). Hospital A kept 23 (6%). Referral Hospital B's Medicare mix suggests that 32% of those stays need a tertiary hospital and 45% are serious medical cases that a 15-bed CAH without an ICU usually transfers. About 22%, roughly 60 stays a year, are conditions a CAH commonly keeps. A digital front door helps where the hospital already has the service and the patient leaves for lack of a call back, an appointment or a nudge. Three moves fit that test, and two more are worth planning.

  1. Bring residents home to a swing bed

    Get an alert when a county resident is admitted at Referral Hospital B or Regional Hospital C. An AI-drafted daily list then tells the swing-bed liaison whom to call and when. Each Traditional Medicare resident brought home for a 30-day stay adds about $33K to margin.

    $91K to $360Ka year, at 3 to 12 more stays

  2. Keep ordered imaging, lab, therapy and GI work local

    An AI scheduling and reminder agent works by phone and text. It books CT, mammography, ultrasound, lab, PT and colonoscopy locally and follows up on open orders. The hospital receives about 60% of county residents' Medicare outpatient dollars; the rest goes elsewhere.

    $128K to $513Ka year, at 5% to 20% more volume

  3. Answer the phone after hours, and book by text

    An AI voice and text line books clinic and telehealth visits, answers "do you do that here?" and sends emergencies to 911 or the ER. The hospital has no online booking today, and office hours end at 5 p.m.

  4. Specialty telehealth in the hospital's own clinic

    Start with cardiology and pediatrics, the specialties residents most often say they lack. Tests the specialist orders get booked locally.

Show the numbers

Hospital A's count went from 103 stays in 2016 to 23 in 2025, while Referral Hospital B's went from 153 to 266. The hospital's cost reports show Traditional Medicare acute discharges going from 82 in FY2019 to 10 in FY2025.

What they go for. CMS doesn't publish diagnoses by home ZIP, so Referral Hospital B's own Medicare mix serves as a proxy. Of its 2,361 published Medicare discharges, 22% are medical conditions a CAH commonly keeps, such as pneumonia, UTI, heart failure, COPD and dehydration without major complications. Another 45% are the same conditions with major complications, and 32% are tertiary care (surgery, heart attacks, strokes, cardiac procedures). Applied to the 266 stays there, that is about 60 CAH-appropriate stays a year, or 180 counting the complicated ones.

These counts include Medicare Advantage: at the largest destinations, CMS's service-area counts ran about 2.3 times their fee-for-service discharges. They don't cover commercial, Medicaid or outpatient care.

How county residents use care compared with the state

Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries in the county, 2024: 511 of 1,407. The rest (64%) are in Medicare Advantage.

Emergency and inpatient use run well above the state (1.5 and 1.4 times per beneficiary), a sign of thin primary-care access. After-hours phone and text booking into the hospital's clinic is the front-door answer, and it keeps the primary-care relationship local.

Skilled nursing days run 3.1 times the state's per beneficiary.

Outpatient dollars leak. The hospital's FY2025 Medicare Part B settlement ($978K) equals about 60% of county residents' Medicare fee-for-service outpatient payments ($1.62M). That share is an upper bound, because the hospital also serves non-residents and cost-based payment runs above outpatient prospective rates. At least $646K a year in fee-for-service dollars alone goes to other hospitals.

The swing program already draws regionally. The hospital's FY2025 Medicare swing settlement ($5.38M) is 3.6 times what the county's fee-for-service residents spent on skilled nursing anywhere ($1.50M), so it is the natural place to bring residents home.

Can residents use a digital front door?

21% of county households have no internet (7% statewide). Among residents 65 and over, 71% have a computer and broadband (84% statewide), and 20% have no computer at all. The weakest ZIP is Town C, with 48% of households on broadband. 82% of households have a smartphone, and 13% get online only through a cellular plan.

Build voice and text first, and treat the portal and online booking as a second channel. Rural Medicare beneficiaries in the state used telehealth at 15.7% in 2025 (19.8% urban), so specialty telehealth tends to work better as a visit in the hospital's own clinic room than as a video call from home.

The front door today

What a patient finds on the hospital's website

  • FindThe home page's Outpatient Rehabilitation link returns "Page not found."
  • FindRadiology offers CT around the clock, X-ray, digital mammography and ultrasound. There's no MRI, so MRI volume leaves by necessity.
  • FindGI offers colonoscopy on its own phone line, and a price estimator covers common procedures.
  • AccessThere's no online booking. Every service says to call, through a phone tree and department extensions, and office hours end at 5 p.m.
  • AccessTelehealth visits are booked only by calling the family practice, during weekday hours.
  • AccessPortal sign-up needs an email on file or a call to medical records during business hours.
  • ReturnThe swing-bed page already lists the post-acute needs of patients leaving the referral hospital: joint replacement, vent and trach weaning, stroke, IV antibiotics and wound care.

What the community and the plan already say

  • CHNAMost survey respondents say the county lacks specialists, led by pediatrics, cardiology and orthopedics.
  • CHNAAmong respondents who hit a barrier, not getting an appointment and long waits were common reasons.
  • CHNAMedicare preventable hospital stays run well above the state rate.
  • PlanThe strategic plan calls for an AI tool, a specialty telehealth partner, non-emergency transport, and pediatrics and women's health recruiting.

What it's worth under CAH payment rules

Change in annual operating margin, low to high volume step

Bars run from the low to the high volume step, and the tick marks the middle step. The open circle shows the middle step for swing beds if MA pays $487 a day.

Traditional Medicare pays a CAH 101% of cost, so added Medicare volume pays its share of the fixed costs the hospital carries anyway. One more Traditional Medicare swing day adds $1,084 to margin, and one more acute day adds $1,630.

Medicare Advantage pays its contract rate. An MA swing day adds $900 at $3,180 a day (about 95% of the interim rate), and loses $1,793 at $487. MA acute days use a $3,500 placeholder and add $734.

Outpatient volume lowers the cost-to-charge ratio. That trims what Medicare pays for inpatient ancillaries, and the engine counts it. At the $487 MA rate, the model would need non-Medicare outpatient to pay 112% of charges to match the hospital's books; at $3,180 it needs 83%. These figures use $3,180.

Assumptions: swing stays last 30 days (the county's Medicare skilled-nursing average), and 64% of them are Traditional Medicare, matching the hospital's FY2025 mix. Acute stays last 3.2 days, with 52% Traditional Medicare. 60% of ancillary cost and 75% of nursing-unit cost are treated as fixed. Lever costs (software, staff time) are not netted out; the workbook has a column for them.

Leakage a front door can't fix

These are capacity or partnership decisions. Better engagement won't bring the care back.

  • MRI, surgery and cardiac procedures, along with heart attack and stroke care: the 32% tertiary share above.
  • Dialysis, mental health, pediatrics and obstetrics, where the county lacks them.
  • Transportation to appointments. An AI booking line can schedule rides once a service exists.

Data that would sharpen this

Each of these is a standard report export. Counts by month, service and destination are enough; no patient names are needed.

  • Outgoing referrals and orders by type and destination: sizes move 2.
  • Swing-bed referrals received, accepted and declined, by referring hospital and payer: sizes move 1.
  • ED visits by acuity and hour, and transfers out by reason: sizes moves 3 and 5.
  • Call volume, abandoned calls and third-next-available appointment for the clinic: sizes move 3.
  • MA contract rates for swing and acute care: decides whether move 1 should include MA patients.
