Keeping care local

Where AI patient engagement and a digital front door can keep patients from leaving the county, and what each move is worth under critical access hospital payment rules.

Fictional sample. The hospital, its county and every figure on this page are invented to show what a briefing looks like. A real run builds the same page from CMS and Census files and the hospital's cost reports.

The short answer

Most county residents who need a hospital bed go to Referral Hospital B, 46 miles away. In 2025 it took 283 of the 529 Medicare stays we can count from the county's ZIP codes (53%). Hospital A kept 73 (14%). Referral Hospital B's Medicare mix suggests that 38% of those stays need a tertiary hospital and 37% are serious medical cases that a 22-bed CAH without an ICU usually transfers. About 25%, roughly 71 stays a year, are conditions a CAH commonly keeps. A digital front door helps where the hospital already has the service and the patient leaves for lack of a call back, an appointment or a nudge. Three moves fit that test, and two more are worth planning.

  1. Bring residents home to a swing bed

    Get an alert when a county resident is admitted at Referral Hospital B or Regional Hospital C. An AI-drafted daily list then tells the swing-bed liaison whom to call and when. Each Traditional Medicare resident brought home for a 24-day stay adds about $18K to margin.

    $70K to $276Ka year, at 3 to 12 more stays

  2. Keep ordered imaging, lab, therapy and GI work local

    An AI scheduling and reminder agent works by phone and text. It books CT, ultrasound, mammography, lab, physical therapy and colonoscopy locally and follows up on open orders. The hospital receives about 23% of county residents' Medicare outpatient dollars; the rest goes elsewhere.

    $190K to $763Ka year, at 5% to 20% more volume

  3. Answer the phone after hours, and book by text

    An AI voice and text line books clinic and telehealth visits, answers "do you do that here?" and sends emergencies to 911 or the ER. Booking is by phone only, and the clinic phones close at 4:30 p.m.

    EnablerMakes moves 1, 2 and 4 work

  4. Specialty telehealth in the hospital's own clinic

    Start with orthopedics and dermatology, the specialties residents most often say they lack. Tests the specialist orders get booked locally.

Hospital A's count went from 210 stays in 2016 to 73 in 2025, while Referral Hospital B's went from 225 to 283. The hospital's cost reports show Traditional Medicare acute discharges going from 152 in FY2023 to 123 in FY2025.

What they go for. CMS doesn't publish diagnoses by home ZIP, so Referral Hospital B's own Medicare mix serves as a proxy. Of its 2,329 published Medicare discharges, 25% are medical conditions a CAH commonly keeps, such as pneumonia, UTI, heart failure, COPD and dehydration without major complications. Another 37% are the same conditions with major complications, and 38% are tertiary care (surgery, heart attacks, strokes, cardiac procedures). Applied to the 283 stays there, that is about 71 CAH-appropriate stays a year, or 174 counting the complicated ones.

These counts include Medicare Advantage: at the largest destinations, CMS's service-area counts ran about 2.2 times their fee-for-service discharges. They don't cover commercial, Medicaid or outpatient care.

How county residents use care compared with the state

The county figures cover Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries (2,939 in 2024). By July 2026, 56% of the county's 6,512 beneficiaries with Part A and B were in Medicare Advantage.

Emergency and inpatient use run well above the state (1.3 and 1.2 times per beneficiary), a sign of thin primary-care access. After-hours phone and text booking into the hospital's clinic is the front-door answer, and it keeps the primary-care relationship local.

Outpatient dollars leak. The hospital's FY2025 Medicare Part B settlement ($2.10M) equals about 23% of county residents' Medicare fee-for-service outpatient payments ($8.99M). That share is an upper bound, because the hospital also serves non-residents and cost-based payment runs above outpatient prospective rates. At least $6.90M a year in fee-for-service dollars alone goes to other hospitals.

Skilled nursing leaks too. County fee-for-service residents spent $3.06M on skilled nursing, against the hospital's FY2025 Medicare swing settlement of $2.79M, so there is room to bring residents home to swing beds.

Can residents use a digital front door?

16% of county households have no internet (8% statewide). Among residents 65 and over, 60% have a computer and broadband (81% statewide), and 20% have no computer at all. The weakest ZIP is Town F, with 66% of households on broadband. 81% of households have a smartphone, and 26% get online only through a cellular plan.

Build voice and text first, and treat the portal and online booking as a second channel. In the first quarter of 2026, 6.3% of the state's rural Medicare beneficiaries used telehealth (8.7% urban), down from 6.6% a year earlier. Specialty telehealth tends to work better as a visit in the hospital's own clinic room than as a video call from home.

What it's worth under CAH payment rules

Change in annual operating margin, low to high volume step

Bars run from the low to the high volume step, and the tick marks the middle step. The open circle shows the middle step for swing beds if MA pays $487 a day.

Traditional Medicare pays a CAH 101% of cost, so added Medicare volume pays its share of the fixed costs the hospital carries anyway. One more Traditional Medicare swing day adds $736 to margin, and one more acute day adds $972.

Medicare Advantage pays its contract rate. An MA swing day adds $1,468 at $3,180 a day (about 95% of the interim rate), and loses $1,225 at $487. MA acute days use a $3,500 placeholder and add $1,368.

Outpatient volume lowers the cost-to-charge ratio. That trims what Medicare pays for inpatient ancillaries, and the engine counts it. At the $487 MA rate, the model would need non-Medicare outpatient to pay 44% of charges to match the hospital's books; at $3,180 it needs 38%. These figures use $3,180.

Assumptions: swing stays last 24 days (the county's Medicare skilled-nursing average), and 68% of them are Traditional Medicare, matching the hospital's FY2025 mix. Acute stays last 3.2 days, with 72% Traditional Medicare. 60% of ancillary cost and 75% of nursing-unit cost are treated as fixed. Lever costs (software, staff time) are not netted out; the workbook has a column for them.

Leakage a front door can't fix

These are capacity or partnership decisions. Better engagement won't bring the care back.

  • MRI, surgery and cardiac procedures, along with heart attack and stroke care: the 38% tertiary share above.
  • Dialysis, mental health, pediatrics and obstetrics, where the county lacks them.
  • Transportation to appointments. An AI booking line can schedule rides once a service exists.

Data that would sharpen this

Each of these is a standard report export. Counts by month, service and destination are enough; no patient names are needed.

  • Outgoing referrals and orders by type and destination: sizes move 2.
  • Swing-bed referrals received, accepted and declined, by referring hospital and payer: sizes move 1.
  • ED visits by acuity and hour, and transfers out by reason: sizes moves 3 and 5.
  • Call volume, abandoned calls and third-next-available appointment for the clinic: sizes move 3.
  • MA contract rates for swing and acute care: decides whether move 1 should include MA patients.
